Who we help

Built for organisations that hold a lot of personal data and have little time to read the law.

The Act applies to anyone who decides why and how personal data is processed. We start with two groups who feel that pressure most.

Co-operative banks

Member trust is the business.

Account, KYC, loan and nominee records sit across core banking, branch files and vendor systems. We help you see where that data lives, who can reach it, and what a member can ask of you.

Growing enterprises

Customer data grows faster than process.

Forms, CRMs, spreadsheets and marketing tools collect data from day one. We help you set simple rules for consent, access and vendors before the sprawl gets expensive to untangle.

Security controls

Six safeguards that do most of the protecting.

The Act expects reasonable security safeguards. We help you choose and sequence them, starting where a failure would hurt people most.

Encryption

Scramble personal data stored on servers, laptops and backups, and in transit, so a stolen copy is unreadable.

e.g. loan files, backup disks

Masking

Show only the digits or fields a person needs for their task, and hide the rest on screens and in reports.

e.g. account number ****4821

Access control

Give each role only the records it needs, remove access when people move, and review it on a schedule.

e.g. teller vs. loan officer

Activity logs

Record who opened, changed or exported personal data, and keep those records safe so incidents can be traced.

e.g. who viewed a member file

Backups

Keep copies you can restore, store them apart from the live system, and test the restore, not just the copy.

e.g. daily offsite copy, monthly test

Vendor safeguards

List every supplier that touches personal data, then agree what they must protect and how fast they must tell you about an incident.

e.g. SMS, cloud, app providers

Customer rights and breach response

Be ready for the request and for the bad day.

Two processes people judge you by. One answers the questions they ask. The other decides how you behave when something goes wrong.

What people can ask of you

Each right needs an owner, a way to receive the request, and a way to verify who is asking.

AccessA summary of the personal data you process and who receives it.
CorrectionFix wrong or out-of-date details, and complete missing ones.
ErasureDelete data that is no longer needed, unless a law requires you to keep it.
GrievanceA named contact who receives complaints and replies in time.

When personal data is exposed

Decide the roles and the order of steps before an incident, not during one.

  1. Detect and logStaff know what to report and to whom, the moment something looks wrong.
  2. ContainCut off the access, device or vendor connection that is leaking data.
  3. AssessWork out which data, which people and how serious the harm could be.
  4. NotifyThe Act requires telling the Data Protection Board and each affected person.
  5. Review and fixClose the gap and update the playbook with what you learned.
